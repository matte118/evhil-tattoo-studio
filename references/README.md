# EVHIL Visual References Guide

This folder contains visual references for the EVHIL Tattoo Studio website.

## Source of truth

The visual references complement, but do not replace:

- `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`
- `docs/DESIGN_BRIEF.md`
- `DESIGN.md`
- `AGENTS.md`

If a reference conflicts with the approved creative direction, the briefs and design documentation win.

## Creative direction

**Dark Graffiti Editorial**

Directional balance:

- 50% Graffiti / Street Culture
- 30% Editorial Premium Structure
- 20% Tattoo / Raw Atelier

Graffiti must be perceptible immediately, including above the fold, but the final result must remain premium, readable, professional, and intentional.

Do not copy any reference literally. Extract composition, hierarchy, texture, spacing, treatment, and interaction principles only.

---

# Brand

Use the assets in `references/brand/` as the primary identity reference.

## TAKE

- EVHIL's real logo and symbols.
- Real studio visual language.
- Dark surfaces and high-contrast identity.
- Graffiti and street cues already present in the physical brand.
- Restrained violet / purple accents.
- Raw, tactile, contemporary tattoo-studio atmosphere.

## AVOID

- Redesigning or morphing the EVHIL logo.
- Turning the eye / symbol into a realistic anatomical eye.
- Cyberpunk or sci-fi reinterpretations.
- Excessive neon.
- Cartoon street-art aesthetics.
- Introducing unrelated brand colors.

## WHY

These references establish what is authentically EVHIL. They should anchor all creative decisions before external inspiration is considered.

---

# Composition

Use `references/composition/` for page rhythm, hierarchy, asymmetry, image placement, editorial tension, and responsive composition.

## TAKE

- Strong image-first layouts.
- Asymmetrical editorial composition.
- Large typography used as structure.
- Deliberate negative space.
- Overlaps and cropped elements when they improve tension.
- Portfolio layouts that feel curated rather than templated.
- Strong mobile composition, not merely a collapsed desktop layout.

## AVOID

- Copying another website section-for-section.
- Excessive visual density.
- Generic centered hero + three cards layouts.
- Perfectly symmetrical grid systems everywhere.
- Layout tricks that become unusable on mobile.

## WHY

EVHIL should feel art-directed, not assembled from a generic tattoo-site template.

# Typography

The typography system has four distinct roles.

## 01-graffiti-primary-display.jpg

### TAKE

- Graffiti as a primary expressive brand voice.
- Hand-made energy with strong readability.
- Black-and-white graphic impact.
- Organic irregularity without becoming cartoonish.

### AVOID

- Using graffiti for body copy.
- Overusing it on every heading.
- Copying the exact alphabet or artwork.

### WHY

This establishes the expressive display language that should be visible immediately in the EVHIL experience.

## 02-condensed-structural-display.jpg

### TAKE

- Condensed grotesk typography for structural headings.
- Strong weight contrast.
- Very large page and section titles.
- Editorial hierarchy.

### AVOID

- Making the whole site condensed.
- Sacrificing legibility for density.

### WHY

This is the structural counterpart to the graffiti layer: `ARTISTI`, `PORTFOLIO`, `STUDIO`, `PIERCING`, and similar titles.

## 03-graffiti-layering-treatment.jpg

### TAKE

- Layering.
- Outlines.
- Controlled spray / paint texture.
- Overlapping graphic energy.
- Selective color accents.

### AVOID

- Copying the exact lettering.
- Rainbow palettes.
- Excessive outlines and decorative noise.

### WHY

Use this as a treatment reference, not as a font reference.

## 04-organic-graffiti-secondary.jpg

### TAKE

- Softer organic graffiti forms.
- Secondary expressive moments.
- Contrast against cleaner typography.

### AVOID

- Bubble-letter dominance.
- Playful or childish treatment.

### WHY

Useful as a secondary graffiti language when the primary handstyle would be too aggressive.

## 05-raw-tag-accent.jpg

### TAKE

- Authentic raw tag energy.
- Handwritten annotation feeling.
- Small expressive interventions.

### AVOID

- Large blocks of unreadable tag text.
- Fake graffiti-font appearance.

### WHY

Useful for labels, marks, accents, overlays, and occasional annotations.

## 06-rough-handstyle-accent.jpg

### TAKE

- Rough handstyle.
- Imperfect rhythm.
- Fast, human gesture.

### AVOID

- Using watermarked artwork as production content.
- Reproducing the source artwork.

### WHY

This reinforces the difference between a real hand-drawn mark and a decorative graffiti typeface.

## 07-clean-body-ui-system.webp

### TAKE

- Clean grotesk body typography.
- Multiple weights and widths.
- Strong small-label / metadata potential.
- Clear navigation and CTA typography.
- Editorial italic accents when useful.

### AVOID

- Letting the clean grotesk erase the graffiti identity.
- Too many weights within the same section.

### WHY

This is the functional typography layer for body text, navigation, buttons, labels, captions, and metadata.

# Photography

Photography should feel tactile, close, credible, and art-directed.

AI-generated tattoo work must never be presented as real EVHIL work.

## 01-tattoo-detail-finished.jpg

### TAKE

- Finished tattoo as the hero subject.
- Close crop.
- Visible skin and ink detail.
- Strong contrast.
- Restrained violet environmental light.

### AVOID

- Over-processing skin.
- Fake HDR.
- Making violet the dominant color of every photograph.

### WHY

The tattoo itself is the product and should remain visually central.

## 02-street-lifestyle-night.jpg

### TAKE

- Street / rap cultural energy.
- Night atmosphere.
- Tattooed subject integrated naturally into lifestyle imagery.
- Strong attitude without corporate posing.

### AVOID

- Fashion-editorial posing that feels disconnected from EVHIL.
- Excessive blue/cyan grading.

### WHY

This extends the brand beyond the tattoo chair into its wider street-cultural world.

## 03-studio-graffiti-environment.jpg

### TAKE

- Graffiti integrated into physical space.
- Raw materials.
- Studio as an environment with identity.
- Visual richness without decorative UI overlays.

### AVOID

- Recreating this studio literally.
- Making EVHIL feel messy or unprofessional.

### WHY

This reference describes environmental character, not the exact EVHIL interior.

## 04-sketch-creative-process.jpg

### TAKE

- Design and sketching process.
- Artist concentration.
- Documentary framing.
- Black-and-white potential.

### AVOID

- Staged desk photography.
- Generic stock imagery.

### WHY

The website should communicate that tattooing begins with design, not only execution.

## 05-tattoo-working-process.jpg

### TAKE

- Artist at work.
- Close framing.
- Professional concentration.
- Tools and hands as storytelling elements.
- Strong monochrome treatment.

### AVOID

- Medical or clinical visual language.
- Graphic procedural imagery used for shock value.

### WHY

This communicates craft, competence, and professionalism.

## 06-black-white-tattoo-editorial.jpg

### TAKE

- Premium black-and-white treatment.
- Body as composition.
- Strong crop and visual tension.
- Tattoo integrated into editorial portraiture.

### AVOID

- Glamour imagery disconnected from tattoo culture.
- Excessive retouching.

### WHY

Useful for monochrome editorial moments and higher-end visual pacing.

# Details

These references define specific UI treatments rather than full-page designs.

## 01-navigation-treatment.png

### TAKE

- Oversized navigation.
- Strong black/white contrast.
- Photography integrated into navigation.
- Menu as a designed moment, not a utility overlay.

### AVOID

- Copying the red palette.
- Excessive brutalism.
- Desktop-only interaction.

### WHY

EVHIL navigation should contribute to the brand experience while remaining fast and clear.

## 02-cta-treatment.png

### TAKE

- CTA as a compositional moment.
- Oversized headline.
- Strong contrast between page and action area.
- Clear, highly visible action.

### AVOID

- Copying the long form.
- Copying the red palette.
- Generic SaaS button styling.

### WHY

`Richiedi un preventivo` and `Scrivici su WhatsApp` should feel like major editorial moments, not small utility buttons.

## 03-portfolio-grid-treatment.png

### TAKE

- Image-dominant portfolio.
- Irregular editorial rhythm.
- Minimal interface chrome.
- Black negative space used compositionally.
- Mixed image proportions.

### AVOID

- Copying the exact masonry layout.
- Sacrificing browsing clarity.
- Tiny metadata competing with the work.

### WHY

EVHIL's work should be curated like an art portfolio rather than displayed as a generic card grid.

## 04-footer-contact-treatment.png

### TAKE

- Footer as a final brand moment.
- Large wordmark / typography.
- Clear contact, information, and social columns.
- Generous negative space.
- Strong final visual statement.

### AVOID

- Treating the footer as an afterthought.
- Copying the exact typography or layout.
- Filling the footer with unnecessary links.

### WHY

The final viewport should reinforce EVHIL and make contact/location actions obvious.

# Motion

There is no required `references/motion/` folder.

Use:

- the installed Motion skill;
- the Motion MCP;
- the installed `motion` runtime;
- current official Motion documentation and examples.

## Principles

- Use `scroll()` for continuous scroll-linked animation.
- Use `inView()` for viewport-triggered reveals.
- Prefer CSS transitions when Motion would add unnecessary complexity.
- Use `position: sticky` for pinned storytelling where appropriate.
- Keep parallax controlled.
- Never hijack scrolling.
- Never make essential interactions hover-only.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Motion must support hierarchy, storytelling, navigation, or brand expression.
- Preserve EVHIL brand artwork exactly; animate reveal, masking, texture, opacity, or surrounding elements instead of morphing the logo.

# Global visual rules

## TAKE

- Graffiti visible immediately.
- Premium editorial structure.
- Tattoo photography as a primary visual asset.
- Black / near-black foundations.
- Off-white typography.
- Restrained violet accents.
- Strong typography hierarchy.
- Controlled asymmetry.
- Tactile textures.
- Raw details balanced by professional execution.
- Intentional responsive composition.

## AVOID

- Generic tattoo templates.
- Generic SaaS UI.
- Card-heavy layouts.
- Excessive pills.
- Glassmorphism.
- Cyberpunk neon overload.
- Rainbow graffiti.
- Cartoon graffiti.
- Overly clean luxury minimalism with no street identity.
- Constant animation.
- Scroll hijacking.
- Excessive parallax.
- Decorative motion without purpose.
- Invented artists, testimonials, prices, events, or studio facts.
- AI-generated tattoo imagery presented as real studio work.
